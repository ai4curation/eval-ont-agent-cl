# Update hypertrophic chondrocyte term (CL_0000743)

## Summary

This PR updates the hypertrophic chondrocyte term to reflect modern understanding based on genetic lineage tracing studies, which have shown that these cells are not terminally differentiated but can transdifferentiate into osteoblasts and osteocytes.

## Changes Made

### Definition Update
**Previous definition:**
> "Chondrocyte that is terminally differentiated, produces type X collagen, is large in size, and often associated with the replacement of cartilage by bone (endochondral ossification)."

**New definition:**
> "A chondrocyte that is part of the hypertrophic cartilage zone. This cell is significantly enlarged and characterised by high expression of type X collagen (COL10A1) in both humans and mice. It actively coordinates endochondral ossification by mineralising the extracellular matrix, attracting blood vessels via angiogenic signalling, and mediating the transition from cartilage to bone - often by transdifferentiating into an osteoblast rather than undergoing apoptosis."

## Background Research

Modern genetic lineage tracing has confirmed that hypertrophic chondrocytes:
- Do not simply undergo apoptosis as historically believed
- Survive and cross the cartilage-bone boundary
- Can dedifferentiate into skeletal stem/progenitor cells (SSPCs)
- Redifferentiate into osteoblasts and osteocytes
- Are a major source of trabecular osteoblasts and marrow adipocytes (per single-cell RNA-seq)

This updated definition better reflects the biological reality demonstrated in PMID:35179487 and related research.
