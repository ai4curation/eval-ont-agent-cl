### New Term Details
- **ID**: CL_9900001
- **Label**: quiescent fibroblast
- **Parent**: fibroblast (CL_0000057)
- **Synonym**: inactive fibroblast (with reference PMID:22529592)

### Definition

"A fibroblast in a quiescent state, characterized by a smaller, spindle-shaped morphology with a relatively small cytoplasm, modest rough endoplasmic reticulum and condensed chromatin. Despite low proliferation and contractility, it maintains high metabolic activity for extracellular-matrix homeostasis through continuous matrix protein turnover and mechanosensitive signaling. This cell can rapidly differentiate into contractile myofibroblasts under injury or inflammatory cues to drive tissue repair."

## Rationale

This term addresses a long-standing distinction in fibroblast biology. While fibroblasts were historically separated into "quiescent" and "fibrocyte" categories, modern understanding recognizes that all uninjured fibroblasts exist in a quiescent state, while fibrocytes are a distinct population. This term now provides explicit representation of the quiescent fibroblast phenotype, with emphasis on:

1. Its specific morphological characteristics (spindle-shaped, small cytoplasm)
2. Its functional properties (high metabolic activity despite low proliferation)
3. Its plastic nature (ability to transition to myofibroblasts under appropriate signals)

This addresses issue #3252 and provides a standardized term for the research community.
